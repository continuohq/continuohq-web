## The short answer: yes, say it in the first message

If an AI assistant answers your customers on WhatsApp, Instagram or your website, the safest approach today is for it to say so clearly at the very start of the conversation. Three separate sources point the same way: the European Union's AI Act, Meta's messaging policy and the generative AI guide from Türkiye's Personal Data Protection Authority (KVKK). Below you will find what each says, how far it concerns a business in Türkiye, and ready-to-use example messages.

## What does Article 50 of the EU AI Act say?

Article 50(1) of the EU AI Act (Regulation (EU) 2024/1689) requires AI systems intended to interact directly with people to be designed so that those people are informed they are interacting with an AI system. Under Article 50(5), this information must be given in a clear and distinguishable manner, at the latest at the time of the first interaction. Article 113 states that these provisions apply from 2 August 2026.

The Digital Omnibus on AI, Regulation (EU) 2026/1744, published in the Official Journal in July 2026, changed the timetable for some obligations. The transition period it introduced for Article 50 covers only paragraph 2, the duty to mark the outputs of generative systems in a machine-readable format. Systems placed on the market before 2 August 2026 have until 2 December 2026 for that marking. The chatbot disclosure rule applies as written.

On 20 July 2026 the European Commission published non-binding guidelines on Article 50. The points that matter most for businesses:

- The duty falls first on the **provider**, the party that develops the system or has it developed and puts it into service under its own name. A company that builds a chatbot in-house and uses it under its own name also counts as a provider.
- The exception for cases where AI use is "obvious" is read narrowly. The guidelines list chatbots embedded in online platforms and helpdesk tools, whose replies people may perceive as human-written, among the examples that do not qualify and therefore need disclosure.
- If a staff member takes a draft from an AI tool, reviews it and sends it themselves, that is not direct interaction. Where the assistant's own replies are mixed with staff replies, the AI-generated replies need disclosure.

## Does it apply to a hotel or clinic in Türkiye?

Article 2 of the Act covers providers placing systems on the EU market even when they are established outside the EU. Providers and deployers outside the EU can also be covered where the system's output is used in the Union. The Commission's guidelines add that incidental, unforeseeable or unauthorised use should not alone trigger this.

In practice: if you offer the assistant as part of marketing aimed at guests or patients in the EU, for example on a German-language booking page or in campaigns targeting EU countries, the chance that the rule concerns you rises. The answer depends on how each business is set up, so we recommend getting a lawyer's view on your own case. Adding a disclosure message takes a few sentences, so there is little reason to wait for that answer before adding one.

## What do Meta and WhatsApp require?

Meta's Messenger and Instagram messaging policy says that, when required by applicable law, automated chat experiences must disclose that a person is interacting with an automated service. It names three moments: the beginning of a conversation, after a significant lapse of time, and when a chat moves from a human to an automated experience. It asks chats serving users in California and Germany to pay special attention. Where it is not legally required, Meta still recommends disclosure as best practice.

The WhatsApp Business Messaging Policy allows automation within the 24-hour customer service window and requires prompt, clear and direct escalation paths alongside it: in-chat transfer to a human agent, phone, email, web support, in-store visits or a support form. The policy text has no separate clause on disclosing AI. We cover the wider WhatsApp rules in our [WhatsApp AI assistant rules](/en/blog/whatsapp-ai-assistant-rules) article.

## What does the KVKK guide say?

KVKK's "Generative AI and the Protection of Personal Data Guide (in 15 Questions)", published in November 2025, mentions chatbots in its transparency section. According to the guide, it is important that people talking to these systems clearly know they are communicating with a generative AI system. It describes a mechanism that clearly states the system is AI-based as a point to be taken into account for transparency and user safety. The guide frames this as guidance. The separate duty to inform under Article 10 of Law No. 6698 still applies to personal data.

## What makes a good disclosure message?

The Commission's guidelines treat several methods as insufficient on their own: disclosures only in terms and conditions or behind a link, vague references such as "assistant", misleading human-like representations, general statements such as "services on this website use AI", and technical descriptions such as "this system uses LLMs". The assistant should also answer truthfully when asked whether it is AI.

Examples you can adapt:

- **Hotel:** "Hello, I am the AI assistant for [hotel name]. I can help with availability, prices and facilities. Type 'reception' at any time to reach our team."
- **Clinic:** "Hello, these messages are answered by an AI assistant. I can book appointments and share general information. I will pass any questions about your health to our specialist."
- **Turkish-speaking customer:** "Merhaba, ben [Otel adı] yapay zeka asistanıyım. Dilediğiniz an 'resepsiyon' yazarak ekibimize bağlanabilirsiniz."
- **Staff to assistant handover:** "[Staff name] from our team has finished the conversation. From now on, our AI assistant will reply to your messages."
- **After a long gap:** "Welcome back. Just a reminder that you are chatting with an AI assistant."

## When should you repeat it?

According to the Commission's guidelines, a single, prominent disclosure at the start of a conversation is likely to suffice in most cases. In contexts with a higher risk of being misled, such as health advice and complaints handling, periodic reminders are likely to be necessary.

| Situation | What to do |
|---|---|
| A new conversation starts | Say it is AI in the first message |
| A staff member hands over to the assistant | Announce the switch in one sentence |
| The customer writes again after a long gap | Repeat the disclosure, setting the interval yourself |
| "Am I talking to a real person?" | Say clearly that it is AI and offer a way to reach a person |
| Health, complaints, refunds | Remind them and hand the topic to the team |

## A short checklist before launch

1. Does the assistant's first message clearly include the words "AI"?
2. Do the profile name and picture suggest a human?
3. Is there an automatic disclosure line when staff hand over to the assistant?
4. Does the customer know how to reach a person when they want to?
5. Are English and, where relevant, German versions of the message ready for foreign guests?
6. Does your privacy notice cover the data the assistant processes?
